Research/Human–Robot Collaboration

Shared Autonomy, Human–Robot Collaboration and Robotic Hands

Robots that work with people must understand what they intend and share control with them in a natural way. We decode muscle activity to control robotic hands and to teach collaborative robots, let the robot regulate grip strength in shared autonomy, map human hand motions onto anthropomorphic hands, and build wearable devices that assist the human arm.

Myoelectric Control of Robotic Hands

4 papers

Surface electromyography (sEMG) measured on the forearm reveals how a person intends to grasp. We regress these signals into continuous commands for multi-fingered robotic hands while avoiding the tedious instant-by-instant labelling that supervised learning requires: non-negative matrix factorization combined with deep neural networks in a self-supervised scheme, minimally supervised networks trained with a soft-DTW loss, weakly supervised regression, and temporal convolutional networks that run on a low-power microcontroller.

02

Shared Autonomy and Grip-Strength Regulation

5 papers

Holding an object firmly but gently is hard when the command is a noisy muscle signal and the user cannot feel the contact. In our shared-autonomy schemes the robot takes over part of the control: tactile sensors on the fingers close the force loop, vibrotactile feedback tells the user how strongly the hand is gripping, and hidden Markov models applied to tactile data split the grasp into phases that set how much autonomy the robot hand takes. Fuzzy force controllers are tuned to each user's preferences. The same idea supports teleoperated cable manipulation with visual aid, and we study EEG error-related potentials as a feedback channel from the user to the robot.

03

Robot Teaching with Neuromuscular Interfaces

5 papers

Kinesthetic teaching lets an operator program a collaborative robot by guiding it by hand, but it records only the path. We add a second channel: a wearable sEMG interface estimates how much the operator stiffens the hand (muscle co-contraction) without interfering with voluntary movements, and vibrotactile feedback makes this level perceivable. Stiffening the hand can request a more accurate trajectory, learned through constrained smoothing B-splines, or set the compliance of the robot point by point, in both kinesthetic and teleoperated programming by demonstration.

Human-to-Robot Hand Mapping and Anthropomorphic Hands

6 papers

To teleoperate an anthropomorphic robotic hand, the motion of the operator's hand must be mapped onto a hand with different kinematics. We reviewed and classified the mapping methods proposed in the literature, and developed hybrid joint–Cartesian mappings that exploit in-hand spatial information to preserve both the finger shapes and the fingertip positions. This builds on the lab's experience with anthropomorphic hands, from the UB Hand to synergy-based control with fingertip force sensors and a compliant, low-cost prosthetic hand.

Assistive Wearable Devices and Safe Physical Interaction

5 papers

Muscle signals also drive devices that physically support the human body. We developed a lightweight soft exosuit actuated by twisted string actuators that assists the elbow in single- and dual-arm tasks, and human-in-the-loop controllers that let an assistive robot either compensate muscular effort or generate it for strength training. For safe physical interaction we designed variable-stiffness actuators, including an anthropomorphic elbow joint, and a twisted-string haptic interface for teleoperation with force feedback.
