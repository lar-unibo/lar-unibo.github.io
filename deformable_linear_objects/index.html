Research/Deformable Linear Objects

Robotic Perception and Manipulation of Deformable Linear Objects

At LAR, we are actively researching approaches to effectively perceive DLOs using deep learning methods, while also developing smart strategies to enable the automatic generation of training datasets. For the robotic manipulation, our focus is on combining both model-based and learning-based approaches, with the aim of addressing some of their individual limitations.

02

Vision-based Dataset Generation

4 papers

Deep-learning perception of deformable linear objects needs large labelled datasets, but annotating thin, deformable and often overlapping cables by hand is slow and error-prone. We develop strategies that generate training data with little manual effort: chroma-key acquisition with background replacement to obtain domain-independent images, semi-automatic labelling with a VR tracker that blends real and synthetic samples, dense annotations obtained from a few sparse inputs, and copy-and-paste augmentation for wiring-harness components.

03

2D Perception

6 papers

Cables and wires lack a distinctive shape, colour or texture, can take countless configurations and often cross each other, which makes them hard to segment for general-purpose detectors. We developed a family of instance-segmentation methods that combine deep networks for semantic segmentation with graph-based reasoning, separating each cable and describing it with a spline: Ariadne+ covers the binary mask with paths on a graph, FASTDLO solves the intersections with skeletonization and a similarity network at more than 20 FPS, and RT-DLO reasons on a graph of centre-line nodes in real time. DLO Perceiver segments the specific cable described by a text prompt, and we also estimate the orientation of the connector at the cable end from a single camera.

04

3D Perception

3 papers

Grasping and manipulating cables requires knowing their shape in 3D. We estimate and track the 3D shape of deformable linear objects from multiple 2D views acquired by a compact camera mounted on the robot: key points extracted from each image are modelled with B-spline curves and matched across views with a multi-view stereo approach, reaching sub-millimetre accuracy, and multi-view model-based visual tracking closes the loop for the robotic manipulation of the cables.

05

Manipulation

7 papers

Manipulating cables is hard because of their unpredictable configuration, high-dimensional state and complex nonlinear dynamics. We combine model-based and learning-based approaches: neural-network models of the cable whose physical parameters are estimated online while the robot controls its shape, spline-based dynamic models, learning-based pipelines that reshape a cable in cluttered scenes, and shared-autonomy frameworks in which the robot assists a human operator.

Robotic Assembly and Industrial Applications
